Belsham — History of Great Britain, Vol. VI

William Belsham (1752–1827) — History of Great Britain, from the Revolution, 1688, to the Conclusion of the Treaty of Amiens, 1802. — In Twelve Volumes. Volume VI. Printed for R. Phillips, London, 1805.

This is Volume VI of Belsham’s 12-volume History of Great Britain, covering the American Revolutionary crisis and the first years of the war (1768–1778) . It fills the 1775–1778 gap between Belsham Memoirs Vol. II (ending 1774) and Belsham Memoirs Vol. III (starting 1779).

The volume contains 572 page-files (OCR’d from the University of Wisconsin / Waukesha Public Library copy, barcode 89095774782), extracted as 20,336 lines (~840 KB) of plain text.

Scope: Covers 1768–1778 — from the Boston Massacre and the Townshend crisis through the Declaration of Independence, the campaign of 1776–77, and the surrender of Burgoyne at Saratoga, to the French alliance and the opening of the global war.

Book-by-Book Summary

BOOK XVI — The Road to Revolution (1768–1774)

  • Review of American transactions — the deterioration of relations after the Townshend duties
  • The Massachusetts Circular Letter (1768) — the assembly’s defiance, Lord Hillsborough’s demand to rescind, the dissolution of the assembly
  • The occupation of Boston by British troops (1768) — 14 ships of war in the harbour, troops landing with bayonets fixed
  • The Virginia Resolves and the non-importation agreements
  • The partial repeal of the Townshend duties — Lord North’s retention of the tea duty
  • The Boston Massacre (March 1770) — the killing of five civilians, the trial of Captain Preston, the defence by John Adams
  • The Gaspee affair (1772) — the burning of the British revenue schooner
  • The Committees of Correspondence — the colonial network of resistance
  • The Hutchinson letters affair — Franklin’s exposure of the governor’s private correspondence, Wedderburn’s attack on Franklin
  • The Tea Act and the Boston Tea Party (December 1773) — the destruction of 342 chests
  • The Coercive Acts (1774) — the Boston Port Act, the Massachusetts Government Act, the Administration of Justice Act
  • The Quebec Act (1774) — extending Quebec’s boundaries, the colonial fury
  • The First Continental Congress — the Declaration of Rights, the Continental Association
  • Lord Chatham’s and Burke’s speeches for conciliation
  • The Petition to the King from the City of London presented by Wilkes

BOOK XVII — The Outbreak of War (1775–1776)

  • Hostilities commenced at Lexington (April 1775) — the shot heard round the world
  • The Battle of Bunker Hill (June 1775) — the British Pyrrhic victory
  • Lord North’s conciliatory proposition — his offer to exempt any colony from taxation if it contributed to imperial defence
  • Burke’s conciliatory proposition — his great speech on conciliation with America
  • The Congress Petition rejected — the Olive Branch Petition
  • America declared to be in rebellion — the Prohibitory Act, the closure of American trade
  • The Declaration of Independence (July 4, 1776)
  • The British campaign of 1776 — the evacuation of Boston, the capture of New York, the Battle of Long Island
  • The Battle of Trenton and Princeton — Washington’s counter-stroke

BOOK XVIII — The War Expands (1776–1778)

  • Military operations in the Jerseys — the campaign of 1777
  • General Howe’s expedition to the Chesapeake — the Battle of Brandywine, the capture of Philadelphia
  • The second engagement at German-Town
  • General Burgoyne’s campaign from Canada — the capture of Ticonderoga, the advance to the North River
  • The disasters at Bennington and Fort Stanwix
  • The surrender of Burgoyne at Saratoga (October 1777) — the turning point of the war
  • The French alliance (1778) — the Treaty of Alliance between France and America
  • The British evacuation of Philadelphia
  • The opening of the global war — France enters the conflict
  • The war with Spain and the siege of Gibraltar — the Spanish entry into the war, the great siege begins

The volume bridges the gap between Memoirs Vol. II (which covers the political crisis of 1767–1774) and Memoirs Vol. III (which starts in 1779 with the later war years). It covers the actual military campaigns of the first phase of the American Revolutionary War — Lexington, Bunker Hill, the Declaration, Trenton, Brandywine, Saratoga — and the French intervention that transformed the conflict into a global war.
