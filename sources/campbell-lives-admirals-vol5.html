Campbell — Lives of the British Admirals, Vol. V (New Edition)

John Campbell (1708–1775), continued by Dr. Berkenhout (to 1779) and Henry Redhead Yorke (to 1780), with a further continuation to the expedition against Algiers (1816). — Lives of the British Admirals: Containing an Accurate Naval History from the Earliest Periods. A New Edition, Revised, Corrected. — Volume V. Printed for Baldwyn and Co., Corner of St. Paul’s Church-Yard, London, 1817.

This is Volume V of the revised “New Edition” of Campbell’s naval history, published as Lives of the British Admirals. It covers from 1756 through 1779, picking up where Vol. IV’s Chapter III left off (the Seven Years’ War) and continuing past the Peace of Paris (1763) into the reign of George III, the growing tensions with the American colonies, and the early years of the American Revolutionary War.

The volume contains 546 page-files (OCR’d from the University of Michigan copy, barcode 319510021664121), extracted as 22,410 lines (~1 MB) of plain text. The printed volume runs to 412+ pages of content.

Scope: This volume is the most dramatic in the series so far — it covers the climactic naval campaigns of the Seven Years’ War (the Battle of Quiberon Bay, the fall of Quebec, the capture of Havana), the uneasy peace of 1763, the deterioration of relations with the American colonies, and the outbreak of the American Revolutionary War. It is the first source in this wiki that reaches into the 1770s.

Chapter-by-Chapter Summary

CHAP. I — George II, to the End of the War in 1763 (line 72, ~8,828 lines)

Continues directly from Vol. IV’s Chapter III. Opens in 1756 with the French threat to Minorca and covers the full sweep of the Seven Years’ War. Key topics:

  • The loss of Minorca (1756) — the French siege of Fort St. Philip, Admiral Byng’s arrival with the relief fleet, the indecisive engagement off Minorca, Byng’s controversial retreat, the fall of the garrison
  • Admiral Byng’s court-martial and execution — the most famous court-martial in Royal Navy history, Byng’s conviction under the 12th Article of War for failing to do his utmost against the enemy, his execution by firing squad on the Monarch (March 1757), and Voltaire’s famous remark that the British “shoot an admiral from time to time to encourage the others”
  • Naval operations 1756–1757 — the defence of the Channel against the French invasion threat, the Rochefort expedition (1757) under Sir Edward Hawke and General Mordaunt, the court-martial of Mordaunt
  • The rise of Pitt the Elder (1757) — the political shift that transformed British naval strategy, Pitt’s commitment to global amphibious operations
  • Operations in the East Indies (1757–1758) — the Battle of Chandernagore under Admiral Watson and Clive, the British capture of French trading posts
  • The Louisbourg expedition (1758) — Admiral Boscawen’s successful siege, the capture of the French fortress, the opening of the St. Lawrence for the Quebec campaign
  • Mediterranean operations (1758) — Admiral Osborne’s blockade of Toulon and Carthagena, the French defeat off Cape Gatte
  • The Battle of Quiberon Bay (1759) — Sir Edward Hawke’s triumphant destruction of the French Brest fleet in a winter gale, the annus mirabilis of British naval dominance
  • The fall of Quebec (1759) — the naval campaign on the St. Lawrence, Admiral Saunders’ fleet, Wolfe’s victory and death
  • Global operations (1760–1762) — the capture of Belle Île (1761), the seizure of the French West Indies (Martinique, Guadeloupe), the Spanish entry into the war and the capture of Havana (1762), the capture of Manila (1762)
  • The Treaty of Paris (1763) — the terms: Canada, Florida, India, and the Caribbean ceded to Britain, the establishment of British global supremacy
  • The chapter ends with the formal conclusion of the war at Paris on 10 February 1763

CHAP. II — George III, from the End of the War in 1763 to the Year 1779 (line 8,900, ~13,510 lines)

The naval history of Great Britain from the Peace of Paris through the first major phase of the American Revolutionary War. Key topics:

  • The post-war peace and the reduction of the fleet (1763–1770) — the demobilisation of the wartime navy, the economic costs of imperial defence, the challenge of administering a global empire
  • The Stamp Act crisis and the growing American discontent — the naval role in enforcing the Navigation Acts, the stationing of naval squadrons in American waters, the growing friction between the Royal Navy and American colonists
  • The Falkland Islands crisis (1770) — the Spanish seizure of Port Egmont, the British naval mobilisation, the diplomatic resolution
  • The Boston Tea Party and the Coercive Acts (1773–1774) — the naval blockade of Boston, the closure of the port, the quartering of troops
  • The outbreak of the American Revolutionary War (1775) — the Battles of Lexington and Concord, Bunker Hill, the siege of Boston
  • The naval campaign in North America (1775–1776) — the British evacuation of Boston, the capture of New York, the naval support for Howe’s campaigns, the Battle of Long Island
  • The invasion of Canada (1775–1776) — Montgomery’s assault on Quebec, the naval transport of reinforcements up the St. Lawrence, the relief of Quebec
  • The Declaration of Independence (1776) and the transformation of the conflict from a colonial rebellion to a full-scale war
  • The French entry into the war (1778) — the Franco-American alliance, the threat of French naval intervention, the British strategic dilemma
  • The Battle of Ushant (1778) — the indecisive engagement between Keppel and d’Orvilliers, the subsequent court-martial controversies between Keppel and Palliser
  • Naval operations in the West Indies (1778–1779) — the Anglo-French struggle for the sugar islands, the capture of St. Lucia, Dominica, and Grenada
  • The British strategic situation in 1779 — the threat of a Franco-Spanish invasion, the Armada of 1779, the naval dispositions along the Channel coast
  • The volume ends with the situation as of 1779, with a note that the narrative continues in the Yorke continuation
  • Volume ends at “END OF THE FIFTH VOLUME” — printed by W. Smith and Co., King Street, Seven Dials

Yorke Continuation — The TOC lists “Continuation of the Naval History, by Mr. YORKE” starting at page 412. This is Henry Redhead Yorke’s updating continuation that extends the narrative beyond Campbell’s original scope. In this volume, the main text by Campbell/Berkenhout runs through Chap. II (to 1779), and the Yorke continuation (seamlessly integrated in the text) extends the narrative with additional detail on the later events.

Vol. V has a significant overlap with the tail end of Vol. IV (both cover the Seven Years’ War), but Vol. V treats the war in far greater depth — Byng’s execution alone receives detailed treatment, and the entire sweep from 1756 to 1763 is expanded. The real value of this volume is Chapter II, which pushes the wiki’s coverage into the post-1763 era — the peace, the American crisis, and the first years of the Revolutionary War.

What this volume does NOT cover: The rest of the American Revolutionary War (1779–1783), the French Revolutionary Wars, the Napoleonic Wars, and the 1816 Algiers expedition. These would require Volumes VI–VIII.
